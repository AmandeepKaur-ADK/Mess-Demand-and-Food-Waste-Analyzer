# Mess Operations – Field Interview Transcript

## 1. Interview Overview

**Location:** College Mess  
**People Interviewed:** Mess Staff (Cook, Keeper/Bookkeeper, Supervisor)  
**Students Served:** Approximately 450 girls  
**Hostels Covered:** 2 hostels sharing one mess kitchen

## 2. Understanding Mess Operations

### 2.1 How is food prepared?

Food is prepared completely through estimation. The cook considers various factors before deciding the quantity of food to prepare.

These factors include:

- Time of the day
- Day of the week
- Mess menu
- Holidays and vacations
- Examination schedules
- Special events
- Type of dishes being prepared

### 2.2 How many students are served?

Approximately 450 girls from two hostels share the same mess kitchen.

The number of students coming to eat varies depending on the meal.

- Lunch has the highest number of students.
- Dinner has comparatively fewer students.
- Breakfast also has fewer students compared to lunch.

### 2.3 How are food quantities estimated?

The cook estimates the quantity based on experience and several factors.

The type of dishes being served also influences the quantity of other food items.

Examples:

- When egg curry is served for lunch, more rotis are prepared (approximately 8–9 kg).
- When sambhar and rajma are served, more rice is prepared.
- On egg days, approximately 8 kg more wheat is used.
- With chicken, egg curry, or dry vegetables, more rotis are prepared.
- With gravy-based dishes, more rice is consumed.

### 2.4 How are leftovers managed?

The mess staff try to minimise leftover food as much as possible.

However, leftover quantities are not measured.

Instead, staff take photographs of the remaining food after the day is over. These photographs are shared in a group containing relevant authorities, including the mess manager and staff members.

Other observations:

- The largest quantity of food is prepared during lunch.
- More girls attend lunch compared to dinner and breakfast.
- More food is left over during dinner compared to other meals.
- Leftover food is mostly wasted.

### 2.5 Do food shortages occur?

Food shortages are considered unlikely because the cook estimates quantities by considering multiple factors.

However, individual dishes can sometimes finish before others because multiple dishes are served during a meal.

### 2.6 Are daily records maintained?

Yes. The keeper maintains handwritten records for each day.

The process is:

1. The keeper maintains daily handwritten records.
2. These records are shared with the respective mess manager.
3. The mess manager prepares an Excel sheet.
4. Excel sheets are used to compare and evaluate financial profits, track food usage, and manage other records.

### Follow-up Questions Regarding Food Wastage

**1. How frequently does this happen?**

Food leftovers occur regularly, although the quantity varies depending on the meal and the number of students attending. Dinner generally has more leftovers compared to breakfast and lunch.

**2. Approximately how much food remains?**

The exact quantity is not measured. However, staff observe the remaining food after meals and try to minimise it. The amount varies according to the menu and student attendance.

**3. How do you currently measure it?**

There is no systematic measurement of leftover food. Staff take photographs of the remaining food after the day is over and share them in a group containing the mess manager and other concerned staff members.

**4. Does it create a financial loss?**

Yes, leftover food represents a potential financial loss because food is prepared using mess resources but remains unconsumed and is mostly wasted. The exact financial loss is not calculated separately because leftover quantities are not measured.

**5. What do you currently do to prevent it?**

The cook estimates food quantities based on experience and considers several factors, including:

- Number of students expected to attend.
- Meal of the day.
- Day of the week and menu.
- Examination schedules.
- Holidays and special events.
- Type of dishes being prepared.

Staff also try to minimise leftovers by adjusting food quantities according to these factors. Photographs of remaining food are shared with the concerned authorities to keep track of the situation.

## 4. Factors Considered During Food Estimation

During the interview, the following factors were identified:

### 4.1 Day of the Week

The menu for the particular day is considered while estimating food quantities.

### 4.2 Meal of the Day

Lunch has the highest demand, and the largest quantity of food is prepared during this meal.

### 4.3 Events and Holidays

Events and holidays are considered because the number of girls attending the mess may decrease.

### 4.4 Examination Schedule

The timing of examinations and the examination schedule of students are also considered.

### 4.5 Mess Menu

The dishes being prepared influence the quantities of other food items.

Examples:

- Chicken, egg curry and dry vegetables require more rotis.
- Gravy-based dishes increase rice consumption.
- Egg curry during lunch requires approximately 8–9 kg of rotis.
- Sambhar and rajma increase rice requirements.
- Egg days require approximately 8 kg more wheat.

### 4.6 Experience of the Cook and Staff

Food quantities are estimated based on the experience of the cook and other mess staff.

They consider multiple factors mentally before deciding how much food should be prepared.

## 5. Important Fieldwork Observations

One important observation from the fieldwork was that the staff do not consider only the time of the meal or the menu.

They also consider:

- Events
- Holidays
- Examination schedules
- Days of the week
- Number of students expected
- Combinations of dishes being served

The entire estimation process is based on the experience and judgement of the staff.

The mess does not currently use a dedicated tool to estimate food requirements.

The primary concerns identified during the interaction were:

1. Leftover food.
2. Food wastage.
3. Individual dishes running out.
4. Estimating the correct quantity of food for each meal.

---

## 6. Initial Problem Understanding

Food preparation in the mess is based on manual estimation and the experience of the cook and staff.

Although several factors are considered, there is no dedicated tool to estimate the quantity of individual food items required for a particular meal.

The number of students attending varies across breakfast, lunch and dinner. Events, holidays, examinations and menu combinations also influence food consumption.

The staff maintain daily records, but leftover quantities are not systematically measured.

Knowing the approximate food requirements in advance would be useful for the mess staff.

A simple tool that helps estimate food quantities while considering the existing factors could help improve food preparation and reduce leftovers and shortages.
